# Small software and the fixed-cost problem

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

A small business can need sophisticated operations without being able to justify a separate consumer app. The relevant comparison is the lifetime cost of maintaining a useful service against the margin generated by its actual usage.

**Hypothetical economics.** Let F be monthly fixed maintenance and integration cost, m contribution per completed task before those fixed costs, and n monthly completed tasks. Break-even requires n*m >= F, provided m is positive. Sharing a runtime can reduce F; it does not eliminate fulfillment, refunds, support or customer acquisition.

Apple's guidelines illustrate that distribution also comes with ongoing participation requirements. An agent marketplace introduces its own maintenance obligations: schema versions, authorization rules, availability accuracy and incident handling. Cheap code generation does not make these responsibilities disappear.

The strongest small-software opportunity may be a reusable operational substrate with a thin business-specific interface. A repair shop needs its own prices and capacity, but not necessarily its own identity protocol or recovery machinery.

**Proposed study.** Compare a shared-service implementation with a standalone implementation for the same business workflow. Include setup hours, monthly maintenance, support time and completed tasks over a full operating cycle. Count owner time even when no invoice is issued.

**Failure condition.** If shared-platform fees and operational constraints exceed the saved fixed cost, the small business is better served by another channel. This is an industry analysis, not a claim about Walify's current implementation or historical product.
