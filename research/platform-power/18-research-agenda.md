# A measurable thesis for the next software era

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

The central bet is that a shared environment for humans and agents can make many small services easier to discover, authorize and operate. The valuable asset would be dependable coordination across tasks. This is a research hypothesis, not evidence that any named company has already achieved it.

**Three proposed studies.** First, compare standalone and shared-runtime implementations of the same small-business workflow, including maintenance and support costs. Second, compare manual navigation with agent execution under identical provider availability, measuring completion, unauthorized effects and recovery. Third, test identity migration between two clients, measuring lost capability and repeated onboarding.

Pre-register primary outcomes and exclusion rules before collecting data. Report all attempted tasks, including abandonment and ambiguous outcomes. Separate synthetic failure injection from observed production behavior, and keep user consent and sensitive data handling explicit.

**Decision rule.** Expand the platform only when saved coordination cost exceeds added governance and dependency costs. Favor task categories with repeated demand and measurable outcomes. A restaurant booking is one industry example; the same method can study repairs, learning services or administrative workflows.

**Author's vision.** The paper's Walify thesis remains a shared software environment with a personal assistant. This supplement introduces no private implementation details, company performance metrics or claims about the old product.

The long-term prediction is conditional: if reliable execution, portable identity and useful supply reinforce one another, the coordination layer may become strategically important. If independent services achieve equal outcomes with lower dependency, the concentration thesis weakens.

## Evidence boundary

- This note proposes research; it reports no completed user study.
