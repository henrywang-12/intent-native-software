# Local density beats global catalog size

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

Small services often sell capacity bound to a place, time or specialist. A restaurant table, repair appointment or tutoring slot cannot be fulfilled by an arbitrary supplier elsewhere. For these markets, global user count is a poor proxy for liquidity.

**Analytical model.** Define a feasible match as one satisfying location, availability, price, accessibility and user constraints. Local liquidity is the probability that at least one acceptable, trustworthy supplier is available when a request arrives. Adding an unsuitable supplier does not improve this probability.

An integrated environment may reduce the cost of trying local services; Tencent's Mini Program description demonstrates the embedded format, not the existence of liquidity in any particular category.

The launch implication is to concentrate on a narrow set of recurring tasks in a bounded market. Measure fulfilled demand before expanding geographic or category coverage. Expanding prematurely can produce a large-looking directory with a high refusal rate.

**Proposed dashboard.** Report eligible requests, feasible offers, confirmed fulfillment, cancellations, supplier response latency and repeat use by location and time window. Include unsuccessful searches in the denominator. Separate supply shortages from integration failures.

**Counterargument.** Some software services are globally deliverable and do not require geographic density. Their constraints may instead be language, regulation or workflow compatibility. The general principle is constraint density: enough relevant supply inside the user's feasible set, rather than enough logos on a landing page.
