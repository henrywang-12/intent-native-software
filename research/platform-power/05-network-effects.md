# Network effects need a causal mechanism

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

A large platform is not automatically a network effect. Scale economies mean average cost falls with volume. Learning effects mean accumulated experience improves quality. A network effect means one participant's value changes because other participants join. These mechanisms have different implications for competition.

**Two-sided hypothesis.** More reliable suppliers increase the chance that a user's intent can be fulfilled. More active buyers increase a supplier's expected return from maintaining a capability. The reinforcing loop fails if new supply is redundant, low quality or geographically irrelevant.

Weixin's documented embedded-service format supplies an example of an environment in which such feedback could occur. Its product description alone does not estimate the strength or direction of the feedback.

A useful model needs quality-weighted variety rather than raw app count. Ten working appointment services in the user's city may matter more than ten thousand inaccessible catalog entries. Supply also competes for attention; additional listings can impose search and verification costs.

**Proposed identification.** Introduce a new supplier category to randomly selected eligible regions or cohorts, where practical and ethical. Measure retained user activity and supplier participation, controlling for subsidies and marketing. Avoid interpreting a simultaneous advertising campaign as organic feedback.

**Falsifier.** If more supply does not improve successful task completion or retention after subsidies stop, the claimed demand-side loop is weak. A marketplace should earn concentration through useful matching, not infer inevitability from its catalog size.
