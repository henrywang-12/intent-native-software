# The App Store as a bundle of institutions

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

An app store combines software distribution with review, discovery, updates and commercial rules. Apple's published guidelines describe admission and conduct requirements. That document establishes the existence of governance; it does not establish that every decision improves welfare or that a competing store could not provide similar services.

**Analytical decomposition.** Developers purchase access to an installed audience, users receive a legible route to software, and the operator adjudicates participation. Bundling can save search and security effort while also making it difficult to replace only the least attractive component. A developer might value distribution but dislike a payment constraint; a user might value review but prefer another recommendation system.

For an intent-native marketplace, copying a grid of icons misses the institutional layer. Every listed capability needs an owner, a versioned action contract, a failure policy and a way to appeal removal. Review of a screenshot cannot establish that an agent-callable refund or booking operation is reliable.

A proposed audit would sample capabilities at admission and again after provider updates. Measure contract conformance, false rejection, undetected regressions and appeal resolution time. Publish the cost of each control alongside its measured benefit.

**Counterargument.** Excessive gatekeeping can exclude useful niche suppliers and slow experimentation. The design target should be accountable governance with proportionate checks. It should not assume that centralized admission is always superior to user choice or independent certification.
