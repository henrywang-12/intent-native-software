# Platform power is control over a sequence

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

The economically important unit is a completed task: discover a provider, establish eligibility, authorize an action, pay, obtain fulfillment and resolve failure. A platform becomes powerful when it reduces friction across several adjacent steps. Owning a catalog alone is weaker than controlling the path between demand and a trusted outcome.

Apple's June 2026 announcement reports more than $1.4 trillion in App Store ecosystem billings and sales during 2025, with over 90% carrying no Apple commission. This is ecosystem commerce, not Apple's revenue or profit. The announcement is an interested company's presentation of an Analysis Group study; it does not identify how much commerce would disappear without the store.

**Analytical claim.** Control can be exercised through access rules, defaults, ranking, identity and settlement, even when no fee is charged on a particular transaction. These mechanisms should be measured separately. Large transaction volume is insufficient evidence of a monopoly, a causal network effect or superior agent execution.

A useful research design compares matched tasks across native apps, a web flow and an integrated environment. Hold provider quality and prices constant; record discovery time, abandonment, failed authorizations, recovery time and total fees. Separately measure whether users can switch providers after an adverse price change.

**Disconfirming result.** If users routinely bypass the platform at low cost and suppliers retain equivalent demand, apparent scale may coexist with weak control. The opportunity is therefore to remove costly coordination steps; concentration is a possible consequence whose strength still needs measurement.
