# When concentration compounds—and when it fails

*Platform power companion · 4 November 2026 · AI-assisted analytical working note.*

A plausible concentration thesis requires several conditions at once: users repeatedly return, suppliers gain incremental demand, integrations remain reliable, switching is costly enough to matter, and rivals cannot easily replicate the relevant advantages.

**Conditional model.** Integration improves completion; completion encourages repeat use; repeat use attracts suppliers; relevant supply improves completion. Every arrow is an empirical claim. Subsidies can imitate the loop temporarily, and worsening fees or trust can reverse it.

A legal monopoly conclusion requires its own market definition and evidence. The European Commission's April 2025 announcement found Apple in breach of the DMA's anti-steering obligation. That dated enforcement finding should not be turned into a general claim that every large ecosystem is an unlawful monopoly, or a statement about later litigation outcomes.

**Counterforces.** Supplier multihoming, portable identity, interoperable contracts, differentiated preferences and independent distribution can weaken concentration. A locally dominant service may still be replaceable at the assistant layer; a dominant assistant may depend on suppliers it does not control.

**Proposed evidence threshold.** Before predicting durable market power, measure retention without subsidies, supplier net benefit, switching after price increases and the success rate of alternative routes.

The entrepreneurial opportunity is substantial if dependable execution becomes a scarce coordination layer. But starting first does not make monopoly automatic. The defensible thesis is a set of mechanisms and falsifiers, not a guaranteed destination.
